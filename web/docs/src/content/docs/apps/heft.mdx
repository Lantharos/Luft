Heft keeps your notes and textbooks for every subject in one place. Write with a pen as you would on paper, type when that's quicker, highlight and annotate PDF textbooks, and find anything again later. It's in [Schelf](/apps/schelf/), and it never goes online.

## Subjects, notebooks and books

The first screen shows your subjects, such as Mathematics or History, each with its notebooks. A notebook is either one you write in, on paper of your choice, or a **book**: a PDF where every page can be written and highlighted on. The PDF itself is never changed.

- **New subject** adds one with a colour and a notebook to start with.
- **New notebook** under a subject picks its paper: blank, lined, squared, dotted, Cornell or music, in A4, A5, Letter or an endless page, white, cream or dark. An endless page grows longer whenever you write near its end.
- **Add a textbook** in a subject's menu turns a PDF into a book. You can also drop PDFs on the first screen, or open them with Heft from Rover.
- The pen button next to a subject starts a **new page** at the end of its most recent notebook, for when class starts. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>N</kbd> does the same in the subject you're in.
- **Recently written** at the top takes you back to the pages you wrote on last.
- Drag notebooks onto another subject to move them, and subjects to reorder them.
- A subject's menu can **archive** it, for last year's subjects. **Archive** and **Trash** at the bottom bring things back; the trash keeps subjects and notebooks for 30 days.

Open notebooks show as tabs at the top. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Tab</kbd> moves between them and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>W</kbd> closes one.

## Writing

Pick a pen in the toolbar and write. Press the pen you're using again to change its kind, thickness and colour, or keep it as a new pen. Black, blue and red pens and a yellow highlighter are there to start; <kbd>1</kbd> to <kbd>9</kbd> pick them from the keyboard.

- **Pressure** makes lines thicker the harder you press. The fountain pen varies a lot, the ballpoint a little, and the highlighter not at all. Highlighter goes under your writing.
- **Hold the pen still** at the end of a line, a box, a circle, a triangle or an arrow, and it becomes a clean one.
- **The eraser** removes whole strokes, or only what it touches; press it again to choose, and its size. The other end of the pen erases too, and so does a side button set to erase in [Settings → Pen](/desktop/pen/#buttons), at first the upper one.
- **The lasso** picks up ink to move, resize, turn, recolour, copy, cut, duplicate or delete. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>V</kbd> pastes it on the page you're looking at.
- **The ruler** lies over the page for straight lines: drag it with a finger or the mouse, turn it with two fingers, the round handle or the mouse wheel, and lines you start along either edge stay on it. It settles on 0°, 45° and 90° when you turn it close to them, and <kbd>R</kbd> shows or hides it.
- **Add a picture** chooses pictures or takes a photo with the camera; pasting one works too. With the lasso, move or resize a picture, or crop it with the button on its corner. A photo offers to crop straight away.
- <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> undoes, also across pages, and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> redoes. Tap with two fingers to undo and three to redo.

## Typing

Click anywhere on a page with the mouse, or tap it with a finger, and type: the text sits on the paper's lines and wraps at the right edge, and ink and typed text mix freely on the same page. On squared and dotted paper it follows every grid line, or every other one on small squares, and blank pages get lines of their own that don't show. Click into text that's already there to carry on typing in it. **Type** in the toolbar does the same with the pen.

While you type, the toolbar offers headings, bold and lists, and the usual shortcuts work too: start a line with `#` and a space for a heading, `##` for a smaller one, `-` or `*` for a list, `1.` for a numbered list and `>` for a quote. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>B</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>I</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>U</kbd> make text bold, italic or underlined, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> stops typing. Typed text is found by search and saved in PDFs as it looks on the page.

For subjects you mostly type in, turn on **Mostly typed** when you make the notebook, or **Carry typing on to the next page** in its menu. <kbd>Enter</kbd> then keeps going down the page, and when a page is full, typing carries on at the top of the next one, adding a page at the end when needed. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd> starts a new page and puts the caret on it.

## Turning writing into text

With **Turn writing into text** on in **Settings**, a line you write becomes typed text in the same place as soon as you pause, a little under a second at first; the pause can be made longer or shorter. Drawings and shapes stay ink. Writing that carries on a line, or starts the line below, joins the text that's already there. To turn ink into text only now and then, loop it with the lasso and press **Turn into text**, whether or not the setting is on. Undo brings the handwriting back.

You correct the text with the pen, as on paper:

- **Write over a word** to replace it with what you wrote.
- **Draw an accent** over a letter to add it: a ˇ for č, š and ž, a stroke for ć, two dots or a short dash for ë, ä, ö and ü, a hook under c for ç, and a line through d for đ. An accent drawn over the wrong accent replaces it, and one over a B in the middle of a word makes it ß. Accents go on the letters of the languages chosen under **Languages you write in**, though any accent can be added.
- **Cross words out** to delete them.
- **Tap between words**, or draw a ^ under the line, to mark where the next line you write goes in.

Heft reads handwriting on this computer and on its graphics card when it has one, where a line takes a fraction of a second; on the processor it takes a second or more. It needs the same download as handwriting search. If you turn on **Keep my corrections**, what you correct is saved on this computer, never anywhere else, so it can help reading get better later.

Your hand can rest on the screen while you write: while the pen is near, touches are ignored. Two fingers scroll and zoom, and one finger scrolls unless you turn that off. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> and the mouse wheel zoom; <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>0</kbd> fits the page's width.

Everything is saved as you write. There is no save button.

**Settings** at the top right sets the pressure curve, how much shaky lines are smoothed, whether a finger can draw, and whether Heft draws slightly ahead of the pen so fast writing doesn't lag. It also puts the tools along the top or down the side, shows pages one after another or one at a time, and turns on searching handwriting. What the pen's buttons do is set for every app in [Settings → Pen](/desktop/pen/).

Folded into a tablet, Heft puts its tools down the side and makes them bigger, and when you type, it keeps the text box above the on-screen keyboard.

With pages one at a time, scroll on past the end of a page, press <kbd>Page Down</kbd> or use the arrows next to the page number to turn to the next one.

The button at the right of the toolbar writes full screen; <kbd>F11</kbd> or <kbd>Esc</kbd> leaves.

## Pages

The panel on the left shows a notebook's pages. Tap one to go there, drag pages to reorder them or onto a tab to move them to another notebook, and open a page's menu to add pages, duplicate it, delete it, **start a section** there or give it its own **paper**. Sections show in the panel and in search. Deleted pages can be brought back from the notebook's menu.

**Paper** in the toolbar changes the paper of the page you're on, or of every page in the notebook, from blank to lined, squared, dotted, Cornell or music, and its size, colour and line spacing. A page given its own paper keeps it when the notebook's changes. The notebook's menu has **Paper** too.

For books, **Contents** shows the book's table of contents.

## Books

**Pick text** in the toolbar selects printed text by dragging across it. Then **Highlight** marks it with clean bars along each line, **Copy** copies it, and **Add to notes** quotes it in the notebook open beside the book.

A notebook's menu can **open another notebook beside** it, such as your notes next to the textbook. Each side scrolls on its own, and the pair opens together again the next time you open either one in that subject.

Drag picked text from the book over to your notes to quote it where you let go. Ink you loop with the lasso can be dragged across as well; from a book, it comes along as a picture of that part of the page, print and all.

## Search

<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> finds subjects, notebooks and sections, runs commands, and searches everything you typed and every book's text. Accents don't matter: "jednacine" finds "jednačine". Each hit shows its page small, with where the words are marked.

To search what you wrote by hand too, download handwriting search in **Settings** (1.6 GB). Heft then reads your handwriting on this computer, in the background whenever you pause writing for a while, and waits while the battery is below 30% and not charging. It reads Serbian, German, English and Albanian written in Latin letters. Reading takes a few seconds a line, so a full notebook takes a while the first time; after that only pages you change are read again.

## Saving copies

**Save as PDF or picture** in a notebook's menu saves this page, this section or the whole notebook as a PDF, with or without the paper's lines, or this page as a picture. Ink stays sharp in the PDF at any size.

## Where your notes are

Everything is in **Documents/Heft**, a folder for each subject and a `.heft` folder for each notebook, so Rover can show and back them up. A copy of the whole library is also kept every day for two weeks in `~/.local/state/com.lantharos.Heft/backups`.
