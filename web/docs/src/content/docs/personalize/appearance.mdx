## Light and dark

Luft starts dark. Pick **Light**, **Dark** or **Auto**, which switches to dark at sunset and back at sunrise, or at times you choose. The **Dark Style** tile in Quick settings and `dark mode` in Gust switch too.

- **Pure black**, offered while Dark is on, makes dark backgrounds fully black, which saves power on OLED screens.
- The lock screen follows your style, and the login screen shows the style of the person it's about to sign in.

## Corners

**Corners** decides the shape of everything: **Rounded**, or **Square** for windows, the taskbar, Quick Settings, Gust, menus and the Luft apps, down to their buttons and switches. Maximized and snapped windows are square where they meet the edge of the screen or another window, and full-screen windows are square either way.

## Glass

The taskbar, Gust, panels, menus and the sidebars and frames of Luft's apps are glass. **Glass** chooses between four materials for each of them, each shown with a small preview on your wallpaper:

| Material | Looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Luca | Glass that blurs whatever is behind it, windows and videos included, and follows it as it changes |
| Maris | The colour of the surface with a hint of the wallpaper's colours behind it, which shift as a window moves |
| Niko | Glass that blurs whatever is behind it like Luca, evened out to one lightness so the colours behind show through softly and text stays easy to read. This is the default |
| Vesa | Clear liquid glass that barely softens what is behind it and bends it along its edges like a lens. It suits the taskbar best when its pieces float on their own |

Pick one for the **taskbar**, for **Gust, panels and menus**, and for **app windows**, in any mix. Some apps keep the material that suits them, whatever you pick. [See-through windows](/windows/see-through/) have a choice of their own.

**Glass blurs only the wallpaper**, on to start with, has Luca, Niko and Vesa blur the wallpaper instead of everything behind them, which looks the same on an empty desktop and costs almost nothing to draw. Turn it off to see windows and videos through the glass. See-through windows always show the windows behind them. While the power saver is on, they blur only the wallpaper, unless you turn off **Wallpaper only in power saver**.

## Wallpapers

Luft comes with 35 wallpapers, photographs of fog, dunes, mountains, coasts and skies. Pictures and videos you put in `~/Pictures/Wallpapers` join them, and [Magpie](/apps/magpie/) can set any photo as the wallpaper.

- **Folders** inside `~/Pictures/Wallpapers` show up as groups named after the folder, each a row you open to see its wallpapers. Once you have wallpapers of your own, the built-in ones move into a group of their own called Luft.
- The light and dark styles each keep their own wallpaper. Luft starts on Fog for dark and Dunes for light.
- When a picture and your display have different shapes, **drag the preview** to choose which part stays on screen. Luft guesses a good spot for each picture by itself.
- **Fit** chooses between Fill, Fit, Center, Stretch, Tile and spanning every display.
- **Videos** play as live wallpapers. On a laptop, you choose whether they keep playing on battery.

## Accent colour

Luft takes an accent colour from the lower part of your wallpaper, where the taskbar sits, and builds a full palette from it, for light and dark, with readable contrast whatever the picture. The desktop and Luft's apps use it together, so a new wallpaper restyles everything at once. Choose **White** instead for a black and white look, or the colourful swatch to pick your own colour. Luft keeps every accent equally light so text on it stays readable, so you pick its hue and how vivid it is.

### Matching other apps

**Match other apps to the wallpaper** gives other apps the same colours: GTK and libadwaita apps, Qt and KDE apps, and the Ghostty terminal. Turning it off removes them again. [Colours and motion](/developers/desktop/colours-and-motion/) says how the palette is built and how apps can read it themselves.

## App icons

| Style | Looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Original | Each app's own icon. Every one of Luft's own apps has a colour of its own. This is the default |
| Tinted | Every icon drawn as a glyph on a plate in one colour, the accent or one you choose, with its main shape kept white, or near black in the light style |
| Clear | White glyphs on a see-through plate, dark ones in the light style |
| Monochrome | Light and grey glyphs on a dark plate, the other way round in the light style |

The Luft mark on the taskbar is always tinted, whichever style you pick.

## Taskbar

How Gust looks, and the taskbar's look, shape, size and alignment, are in Settings, under **Taskbar and Gust**. See [the taskbar's options](/desktop/taskbar/#options).

## Text and fonts

- **Fonts** picks the interface and monospace fonts. Luft uses Open Runde and Maple Mono. **All fonts** lists every installed font, and removes ones you added.
- **Text size** goes from 80% to 160%.

## Cursors

Luft comes with Plume, in light and dark. Pick a cursor theme and size. **Get more cursors** finds and installs themes from GNOME-Look; themes you installed can be removed again. A downloaded theme only ever brings cursor pictures: Settings downloads it over a secure connection, checks each cursor file and leaves everything else in the download behind.
