Everything here is in Settings, under **Appearance**. The page opens on a preview of your desktop that follows every change; wallpapers, app icons, glass, text and fonts, and the cursor each open from a row under it.

## Light and dark

Luft starts dark. Pick **Light**, **Dark** or **Auto**, which switches to dark at sunset and back at sunrise, or at times you choose. The **Dark Style** tile in Quick settings and `dark mode` in Start switch too.

- **Pure black**, offered while Dark is on, makes dark backgrounds fully black, which saves power on OLED screens.
- The login and lock screens stay dark in both styles.

## Corners

**Corners** decides the shape of everything: **Rounded**, or **Square** for windows, the taskbar, Quick Settings, Start, menus and the Luft apps, down to their buttons and switches. Maximized and snapped windows are square where they meet the edge of the screen or another window, and full-screen windows are square either way.

## Glass

The taskbar, Start, panels, menus and the sidebars of Luft's apps are glass. **Glass** chooses between two materials for each of them:

| Material | Looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Luca | Glass that blurs whatever is behind it, windows and videos included, and follows it as it changes. This is the default |
| Maris | The colour of the surface with a hint of the wallpaper's colours behind it, which shift as a window moves |

Pick one for the **taskbar**, for **Start, panels and menus**, and for **app windows**, in any mix. Some apps keep the material that suits them, whatever you pick.

Turn on **Luca blurs only the wallpaper** to have Luca glass blur the wallpaper instead of everything behind it, which looks the same on an empty desktop and costs almost nothing to draw. While the power saver is on, Luca blurs only the wallpaper, unless you turn off **Wallpaper only in power saver**.

## Wallpapers

Luft comes with 35 wallpapers, photographs of fog, dunes, mountains, coasts and skies. Pictures and videos you put in `~/Pictures/Wallpapers` join them, and [Magpie](/apps/magpie/) can set any photo as the wallpaper.

- **Folders** inside `~/Pictures/Wallpapers` show up as groups named after the folder, each a row you open to see its wallpapers. Once you have wallpapers of your own, the built-in ones move into a group of their own called Luft.
- The light and dark styles each keep their own wallpaper. Luft starts on Fog for dark and Dunes for light.
- When a picture and your display have different shapes, **drag the preview** to choose which part stays on screen. Luft guesses a good spot for each picture by itself.
- **Fit** chooses between Fill, Fit, Center, Stretch, Tile and spanning every display.
- **Videos** play as live wallpapers. On a laptop, you choose whether they keep playing on battery.

## Accent colour

Luft takes an accent colour from the lower part of your wallpaper, where the taskbar sits, and builds a full palette from it, for light and dark, with readable contrast whatever the picture. The desktop and Luft's apps use it together, so a new wallpaper restyles everything at once. Choose **White** instead for a black and white look, or the colourful swatch to pick your own colour. Luft keeps every accent equally light so text on it stays readable, so you pick its hue and how vivid it is.

### Matching other apps

**Match other apps to the wallpaper** gives other apps the same colours: GTK and libadwaita apps, Qt and KDE apps, and the Ghostty terminal. Turning it off removes them again. [Colours and motion](/developers/desktop/colours-and-motion/) says how the palette is built and how apps can read it themselves.

## App icons

| Style | Looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Tinted | Every icon drawn as a two-tone glyph in one colour, the accent or one you choose, on a soft plate. This is the default |
| Clear | White glyphs on a see-through plate, dark ones in the light style |
| Original | Each app's own icon. Luft's own apps and the Luft mark are drawn as their two-tone glyph in the same colour as the text around them |

## Taskbar

How Start looks, and the taskbar's look, shape, size and alignment, are in Settings, under **Taskbar and Start**. See [the taskbar's options](/desktop/taskbar/#options).

## Text and fonts

- **Fonts** picks the interface and monospace fonts. Luft uses Open Runde and Maple Mono. **All fonts** lists every installed font, and removes ones you added.
- **Text size** goes from 80% to 160%.

## Cursors

Pick a cursor theme and size. **Get more cursors** finds and installs themes from GNOME-Look; themes you installed can be removed again. A downloaded theme only ever brings cursor pictures: Settings downloads it over a secure connection, checks each cursor file and leaves everything else in the download behind.
