Luft runs the same way on one computer or on a thousand. An organization can take it as far as it needs:

| You want to | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Decide some settings for everyone, such as the screen lock, updates, the firewall or where apps come from | Hand out [policy files](/organizations/policy/) with your configuration management |
| Add your own software, certificates, settings and policy to every computer | [Build your own Luft](/organizations/your-own-luft/) on top of Luft's, signed with your keys |
| Test each update on a few computers before everyone gets it | Pilot computers follow your test builds, and you [promote](/organizations/your-own-luft/#5-test-on-the-pilot-computers) a build when it's ready |
| Keep everything in-house | Mirror Luft into your own registry, or carry it over on a drive into a network without internet. Builds, updates and computers never reach Luft's servers |

People see what you decided. Settings shows a managed setting as it is and doesn't let them change it, and **System › About** says who manages the computer and lists everything you decided, in plain words.

Your own Luft stays yours: it's signed with keys that never leave your hands, your computers only take updates you signed, and their disks only unlock for startup images you signed.
