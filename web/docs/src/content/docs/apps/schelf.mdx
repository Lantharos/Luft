Schelf is where you get apps: from Flathub, as AppImages, and command-line tools from Fedora. System updates, such as the kernel and drivers, live in [Settings](/system/updates/), so Schelf only deals with apps.

<Shot name="schelf" alt="Schelf's Discover page" />

## Finding apps

- **Discover** shows trending, popular and recently updated apps, and categories.
- **Search** looks through your installed apps, Flathub and command-line tools at once.
- **App pages** have screenshots, release notes, the size, licence and source. Flathub apps explain their permissions in plain words, broadest access first.
- Catalog pages are kept on disk, so browsing works offline.

## Installing and updating

- Downloads queue up, show their progress and can be cancelled.
- **Installed** lists every app with its size and where it came from.
- **Updates** updates every app in one go. Flatpak runtimes are listed separately, as app platforms.
- AppImages update by themselves a few minutes after you sign in, and every six hours after that.

Opening a `.flatpakref`, `.AppImage` or `.rpm` file shows a page explaining it, and where it would go, before anything is installed. [Installing software](/system/software/) explains where each kind goes.

## Command-line tools

Search for a tool, such as `ffmpeg` or `htop`, and Schelf installs it from Fedora into a Toolbox container. Its commands work in [Tern](/apps/tern/) like any other, and its updates appear under Updates.

## In the terminal

`schelf install`, `remove`, `search`, `update`, `list` and `info` do the same from [Tern](/apps/tern/), and `sch` is short for `schelf`. While the app is open, what you start in the terminal shows up in its queue too. [Installing software](/system/software/#in-the-terminal) has the details.
