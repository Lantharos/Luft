The taskbar sits along the bottom of the screen, or along whichever edge you choose in its [options](#options). From left to right in its middle are the Luft mark, which opens [Start](/desktop/start-and-search/), and your apps. On the right are the tray, the status icons that open [Quick settings](/desktop/quick-settings/), and the clock, which opens [Notifications](/desktop/notifications/).

<Shot name="taskbar" alt="The taskbar with pinned apps, status icons and the clock" />

## Apps

Pinned apps come first, then any other app that's running.

- **Dots** under an app show how many windows it has, up to four. The app you're using has wider dots.
- **A badge** counts unread messages, for apps that report them.
- **A progress bar** replaces the dots while an app reports progress, such as a download.
- **A flash** means an app wants your attention. It flashes three times, then stays lit until you look.

Clicking an app opens it, switches to its window, or minimizes the window if it's already in front. When an app has several windows, clicking shows them all to choose from.

Hover over an app, or press the arrow key that points away from the taskbar's edge, such as <kbd>Up</kbd> on the bottom, to see **live previews** of its windows, each with a close button.

## Pinning

- Drag an app from Start onto the taskbar to pin it.
- Drag pinned apps along the taskbar to reorder them.
- Right-click an app for **Pin to taskbar** or **Unpin from taskbar**.

## Right-click menus

On a touchscreen, touch and hold for the same menu.

| Where | What's in it |
| --- | --- |
| An app | Open, New window, the app's own actions, each of its windows, minimize and maximize, pin or unpin, and close |
| The taskbar | Show desktop, System monitor, and Taskbar settings |
| The status icons | Network and Sound settings |
| The clock | Date and time settings |

## The tray

Apps that live in the background, such as chat or sync apps, show up in the tray, a single button with up to four of their icons. Click it to see them all, and click one for its menu.

## Show desktop

The far end of the taskbar, past the clock, is a thin strip. Hover over it to fade every window away for a look at the desktop, and click it to minimize everything; click again to bring them back.

## Options

Right-click the taskbar and choose **Taskbar settings**, or open Settings, Desktop.

<Shot name="settings-desktop-taskbar" alt="The taskbar's options in Settings, with a preview" />

| Option | Choices |
| --- | --- |
| Style | Start as a Menu next to the taskbar, or as [Gust](/desktop/gust/), a search bar in the middle of the screen |
| Show the Start button | When off, <kbd>Super</kbd> still opens Start |
| Search the web with | DuckDuckGo, Google, Bing, Brave Search, Ecosia, Startpage or Kagi |
| Show the taskbar | When off, the clock and status icons stay in a corner, see [without the taskbar](/desktop/gust/#without-the-taskbar) |
| Position | Bottom, Left, Top or Right |
| Alignment | Center, or Left (Top on a taskbar along the left or right edge) |
| Look | Glass (blurred), Solid, Transparent, or Accent (tinted glass) |
| Shape | Edge to edge, or Floating, with rounded ends and a gap around it |
| Size | Small, Medium or Large |
| Hide automatically | Never, Always, or When a window touches it |
| Show pinned apps | When off, pinned apps move into Start instead |
| Only list windows on the current workspace | Each workspace's taskbar shows only its own windows |
| Show on every display | With several displays, a taskbar on each one |
| Only list windows on the same display | Each display's taskbar shows only its own windows |

The taskbar sits on your wallpaper, so it takes its light or dark look from the part of the wallpaper behind it: light over a bright beach or snow, dark over a night sky, and your light or dark style when the wallpaper is in between. Start, quick settings and your apps keep following your style. The clock and status icons pick a light or dark ink, whichever reads better against what's behind the taskbar, so they stay clear on any wallpaper.

On the left or right, apps stack from the top and the clock shows the time over the date. Start, quick settings, notifications and the taskbar's menus open beside the button you used, and notification banners appear in the corner next to the clock.

On a convertible folded into a tablet, the taskbar moves to the bottom and becomes a thin strip that you swipe up for a touch-sized taskbar, see [Tablet mode](/desktop/tablet-mode/).

With several displays, each one gets its own taskbar with Start, its apps and the clock. The tray, status icons and privacy indicator stay on the main display. A taskbar steps aside on a display where an app is full screen.
