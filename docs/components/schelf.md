# Schelf

Schelf ([apps/schelf](../../apps/schelf)) installs and updates apps. Besides its window and the `schelf` command, it does some work on its own after you sign in.

## The catalog

Schelf browses one catalog from schelf.store (`desktop/src/store`), as the store's [client contract](https://github.com/Lantharos/schelf-store/blob/main/docs/clients.md) describes: `index/v1/catalog.json` and the mirrors `index/v1/mirror/flathub.json` and `index/v1/mirror/fedora.json` on `dl.schelf.store`, merged with the store's apps first, then Flathub's, then Fedora's, and each id only once. Every file is checked before it's read: its `.sig` must be made by a store key from `trust/v1/store-keys.json`, which the root key built into Schelf has to have signed. The files are kept in `~/.cache/com.lantharos.schelf/store` with their ETags, used as they are for six hours and asked for again when Schelf opens. Search asks `schelf.store/api/v1/search` and shows only hits the signed catalog has, and searches the catalog on disk when the store can't be reached. Flathub apps' screenshots, descriptions and permissions still come from Flathub's own API, and Fedora apps' details from Fedora's repositories.

A store app's own file, `index/v1/apps/ID.json`, decides what installs: the highest version on the channel you follow whose rollout has reached this computer, with a file Luft installs, and an update is only a higher version on what you follow. Other publishers' apps follow stable unless their page switched them to beta or nightly, which the page offers when the app ships on them. Luft's own apps and extensions, the ids under `com.lantharos.`, go with the system instead: on a nightly Luft they follow nightly, on a stable one stable, and their page offers Same as Luft first. A system on nightly isn't taken as agreeing to anyone else's nightlies. Schelf asks the update service which image the system follows, the same `bootc status` Settings shows, once each time it starts, and the image's tag says the channel; away from Luft's images everything follows stable. Going with a nightly system takes the highest version on nightly and stable together, so an app with no nightly release yet installs its stable one and nothing ever goes backwards. A system moved to another channel changes this default on the next update check: moved from nightly to stable, a copy newer than stable stays as it is, with no downgrade, until a stable release is higher. Choosing a channel on the page beats all of this, stable included, and takes only that channel's releases; the choice is kept in `~/.local/share/com.lantharos.schelf/channels.json`, and Sabine apps follow it too, since Schelf installs their updates. When a publisher ends a channel, the signed file's `ended_channels` says where it points, and whoever follows it moves there, whether by choice or with the system, once the destination has something for this computer. The choice stays in `channels.json`, so reopening the channel brings you back, the page shows the channel you're really on and says why, and a copy newer than the destination stays until the destination is higher. A Sabine release installs from its `archive`, an `appimage` from an AppImage or a `files` release's AppImage, and an extension from its `extension`; a release with other Linux files (`deb`, `rpm`, `flatpak`, archives, programs, or ones for `any` system) only offers the best of them to download, and one with nothing for Linux isn't offered. When an app's newest release installs another way than the installed one, after a move to a new framework, the new one replaces it; schelf.store's bridge releases never reach Luft, since its index leaves them out. A project that isn't in the catalog, such as `publisher.slug`, still opens with `schelf:app/ID` from its own file. The rollout compares the release's percentage with a number drawn once per account and kept in `~/.local/share/com.lantharos.schelf/rollout`. Downloads carry `?reason=install` or `?reason=update`, and a file whose size or SHA-256 differs from the signed entry is thrown away. AppImages install through the AppImage sandbox with the permissions in the signed release, Sabine apps from their Linux archive through `SabineService::install_store_app`, which leaves their updates to Schelf and refuses a release schelf.store pulled, and extensions are unpacked into `~/.local/share/luft/extensions/ID` and loaded with Kestrel's `Reload`, and removed with its `Remove` (see [extensions](../extensions.md)). An extension's release is only offered when Kestrel's `ApiVersions` lists the API version it was built for, and what it may do is shown in the extension API's own words, with a helper as "Runs its own program". `~/.local/share/com.lantharos.schelf/store.json` records each one with its version and the access you agreed to; an update that widens it waits until you agree again. When a publisher deletes an app from schelf.store, its file is signed again with no releases: a copy you have keeps running and can be removed, its page says it's no longer available on Schelf, and no update is offered. An app whose file is gone altogether is shown the same way from what `store.json` recorded. When schelf.store marks a delisted app `replaced_by` another, its page points to that one, and its update moves you over: Schelf installs the replacement with the access you agreed to for the old one, unless you have it already, then removes the old one. A replacement that installs another way, or asks for more, waits until you agree to it. An extension whose manifest says it `replaces` the old one gets its settings from Kestrel, which also takes the old folder away on its own.

`schelf --check-revocations` runs at sign-in and every hour (`luft-app-revocations.timer`), and Schelf checks too while it's open. It accepts `trust/v1/revocations.json` only when it's signed and its serial isn't lower than the last one, and writes the AppImages of pulled store apps to `~/.local/share/luft/blocked-appimages.json`. Their sandbox refuses to start them and opens their page in Schelf, which explains why and offers to remove them.

## Preinstalled apps and background updates

On Luft, `schelf --preinstall` runs in the background as soon as the desktop is up and adds what the image keeps in `/usr/share/luft/preinstall` to the account, once each, so a removed app stays removed. Each AppImage there, such as Helium, installs under the app ID its file is named after, such as `net.imput.helium`. Each `ID.json` is a store app's listing holding only the release the image ships, next to that release's file: it installs the way a download from schelf.store would, without going online, unless it's already installed from the store, and from then on Schelf updates it like any other store app. A file whose size or SHA-256 differs from the listing isn't installed. The names done are kept in `~/.local/state/luft/preinstalled`. `schelf --update-apps` runs a few minutes after sign-in and every six hours after that, and updates every store app whose new release doesn't ask for more than you agreed to, and every other AppImage with a newer version. `schelf --integrate-apps` runs once the desktop is up after sign-in, whenever Schelf starts and after it installs or updates a Flatpak: it repairs AppImage launchers and turns on Chromium's own fix for touchpad scrolling on Wayland in Chromium-based Flatpaks whose launcher reads a flags file, such as Chrome, Chromium, Brave, Edge, Vivaldi and Discord. Chromium has that fix from version 154; Kestrel scrolls apps built on older Chromium at the fingers' pace itself.

## The AppImage sandbox

The same binary starts AppImages in their sandbox: menu entries run `schelf --sandbox APP-ID`, which `apps/schelf/sandbox` (`luft-sandbox`) implements. The first launch of an app mounts its image with `squashfuse`, starts `xdg-dbus-proxy`, opens a Wayland security context and starts bubblewrap; the sandbox's own server inside then starts the app, and later launches hand it their arguments over a socket in `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/luft-sandbox/APP-ID`, so all windows of an app share one sandbox. Its bus goes through a relay in the launcher that answers Flatpak's Spawn portal, which starts the nested sandboxes Chromium and Electron run their helpers in, through Zypak in `/usr/lib/zypak`. What the sandbox holds and why is in [The AppImage sandbox](../security.md#the-appimage-sandbox), and how apps declare what they need in [App sandbox](../app-sandbox.md). `schelf:full-access?APP-ID` opens an installed AppImage's or Flatpak's page with the full access confirmation, which the sandbox's notifications and Settings → Apps link to.
