Disks shows the drives in your computer and the ones you plug in. Each drive is one bar with its partitions to scale, and each partition has the action you most likely want next to it: Open, Mount or Unlock.

<Shot name="disks-editor" alt="The partition editor in Disks, with a new partition planned" />

## What it does

- **Mount and unmount**, rename, open in [Rover](/apps/rover/), and choose whether a partition mounts at startup, at a folder you pick. Data drives inside the computer [open on their own](/apps/rover/#drives) when you sign in.
- **See what's using space** on a partition or in any folder, as a map and a sorted list, and move things to the trash from there.
- **Format by purpose**: *All computers* (exFAT), *Linux only* (ext4), or *Other* for Btrfs, NTFS and FAT32. Optionally encrypt it with a passphrase, or overwrite old data.
- **Edit partitions**: create, delete, grow, shrink and move them. Changes collect as a plan you can undo, and apply in one step.
- **Encryption**: unlock, lock, change the passphrase, and turn encryption on in place without losing data, with a recovery key. Encrypting runs in the background and can be paused. See [Security](/system/security/#other-drives).
- **Health** and self-tests for SATA and NVMe drives.
- **Disk images**: save a drive or partition as an image, write one back, and make a bootable USB stick from an `.iso` file. Opening an `.iso` in Rover offers this.
- **Safely remove** USB drives.
- **Other systems**: Windows and other Linux systems show up below their drive's partitions, and can be removed to give Luft their space. See [Removing Windows or the old system](/system/remove-other-systems/).
- **Move Luft to another drive**, such as a faster one, with your files, apps and settings, from the menu of the drive Luft runs from. See [Moving Luft to another drive](/system/move-to-another-drive/).

Anything that erases data asks first. You can look at the drive Luft runs from, but the partitions it uses can't be changed, except that Luft itself can grow, shrink and move. Changes that need an administrator ask for your password.

## Giving Luft more space

Free space on the drive Luft runs from has **Give to Luft** next to it.

- **Right after Luft**, Luft grows into it at once, while you keep working. When Luft is encrypted and the TPM doesn't unlock it, Disks asks for its passphrase or recovery key.
- **Right in front of Luft**, as when Windows gave up some space, Luft moves into it the next time the computer starts, then grows over any free space after it too. Moving copies all of Luft's files, which takes a few minutes, or up to an hour or more on a full drive, and you can use the computer meanwhile. Keep it plugged in. If it turns off partway, Luft carries on where it left off at the next start. Until then the free space says Luft moves in at the next start, and **Keep it free** calls it off. Dragging Luft's left edge into the space in the partition editor does the same.
- **Behind one other partition**, such as a data partition, Disks moves that partition to the other side of the free space first, then gives Luft the space. Partitions that belong to Windows or another system, that the computer needs to start, or that are in use stay where they are, and Disks says so.

Luft needs room to move: a Luft that's very full can only move into a space bigger than what its files take up. An encrypted Luft that only opens with a passphrase can move as long as you type the passphrase when the computer starts. Luft stays on one drive, so free space on another drive can only become a partition of its own, or Luft can [move to that drive](/system/move-to-another-drive/) altogether.
