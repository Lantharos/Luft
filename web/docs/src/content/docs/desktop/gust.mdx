Press <kbd>Super</kbd> or click the Luft mark on the taskbar to open Gust. It finds your apps, open windows, settings, files and quick answers as you type. Settings, under Input, **Keyboard shortcuts** can add a shortcut for **Open Gust** next to <kbd>Super</kbd>.

## Two looks

Choose how Gust looks in Settings, Taskbar and Gust, under **Gust**. Both search the same things, in the same order.

| Look | Opens |
| --- | --- |
| Panel | Next to the taskbar, with the search field at the top, your pinned apps below it, and your account and power buttons at the bottom |
| Palette | In the middle of the screen, with every app and your open windows until you type |

## Pinned apps and folders

The panel shows the apps you pinned to Gust, starting with a few of Luft's own and your browser. These pins are Gust's own; the taskbar keeps its own. Resting the pointer on an app says what kind of app it is, such as File Manager for Rover.

- **Pin an app** with **Pin to Gust** in its right-click menu, in all apps or in search results. On a touchscreen, hold it instead. It goes at the end.
- **Unpin an app** with **Unpin from Gust** in its menu.
- **New apps** you install are pinned at the end by themselves. Turn off **Pin new apps** in Settings, Taskbar and Gust, to pin them yourself.
- **Rearrange** apps by dragging them.
- **Make a folder** by dragging one app onto another. Name it in the field at the top of the folder.
- **Take an app out** of a folder by dragging it onto **Pinned** at the top, or with **Move out of folder** in its menu. A folder that's empty disappears.
- **Pin an app to the taskbar** by dragging it there, or with **Pin to taskbar** in its menu.

## All apps

The button next to the search field in the panel lists every app from A to Z, with a small pin next to the ones you pinned; it, or <kbd>Esc</kbd>, goes back to your pinned apps.

The palette shows every app before you type, with categories such as Productivity, Internet and Games along the top. **Open**, right after All, lists your open windows with a preview of each. Typing the start of an app's name narrows the apps down. Anything else, such as a calculation, a file, a setting or a window, switches to the full results, with the best match on top. Arrow keys move through the apps, <kbd>Enter</kbd> opens the highlighted one, and <kbd>Tab</kbd> moves to the categories, where the arrow keys pick one.

## Shutting down, restarting and signing out

The power button at the bottom of the panel shows Lock, Sleep, Sign out, Restart and Power off, and in either look you can type them. Restart, Power off and Sign out ask once before they go ahead, say that open apps will close, and go ahead by themselves after a minute unless you cancel.

## Search

The best match sits on top and <kbd>Enter</kbd> opens it; the rest follow in groups.

<Shot name="gust-search" alt="Gust working out a calculation" />

| What | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Apps, by name, kind and keywords | `mail`, `files`, `terminal`. Apps you open often rank higher |
| What apps can do, from their menus | `private window`, `new window` |
| Open windows, by title or app | Picking one switches to it |
| Calculations | `2*(3+18)`, `15% of 80`, `80 + 15%`, `2^10`, `sqrt(16)`, `7 mod 3`. <kbd>Enter</kbd> copies the result |
| Answers about dates, times and money | `days until christmas`, `time in tokyo`, `100 usd in eur`, see [quick answers](#quick-answers) |
| Unit conversions | `10 km to mi`, `100 f`, `1.5 GB in MiB`, `90 min`. Without a target unit, Gust picks the usual one |
| Actions | `dark mode`, `night light`, `dnd`, `wifi off`, `bluetooth`, `airplane mode`, `keep awake`, `lock`, `sleep`, `sign out`, `restart`, `shut down` |
| Settings pages and sections | `night light`, `vpn`, `taskbar` |
| Recent files | The same list as Recent in Rover |
| Files and folders in your home folder | Several words narrow it down by folder, so `garden readme` finds `garden-planner/README.md` |
| Text inside files | Plain text, Markdown, code and other text files up to 1 MB, and the first 40 pages of PDFs. The matching line shows under the file |
| Clipboard history | Text you copied, by what it says, or everything with `clipboard`. Picking it pastes it |
| Emoji | `grinning`, `thumbs up`. Picking one types it |
| The web | Always last, and first when nothing else matches. Settings chooses the search engine |

Conversions cover length, mass, temperature, data sizes, time, volume and speed. Sign out, restart and power off still ask first.

| Key | Does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Opens the best match, or the highlighted result |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd> | Shows the file in Rover |
| <kbd>Down</kbd> or <kbd>Tab</kbd> | Moves into the results in the panel |
| <kbd>Up</kbd> and <kbd>Down</kbd>, <kbd>Page Up</kbd> and <kbd>Page Down</kbd> | Move through the results in the palette, while the search field keeps the keyboard; <kbd>Left</kbd> and <kbd>Right</kbd> also move through its apps |
| <kbd>Menu</kbd>, <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>F10</kbd> or right-click | More for a result, such as **Show in Rover** or **Copy path** |
| <kbd>Esc</kbd> | Clears the search, then leaves a folder, all apps or a category, then closes Gust |

In the palette, the highlighted result shows a preview on the right when it has one: a live picture of a window, an emoji at full size, a picture or text from the clipboard history, or the answer to a question.

## Quick answers

Ask about dates, times and numbers in plain words. The answer comes up first, and <kbd>Enter</kbd> copies it.

<Shot name="gust-answers" alt="Gust answering what time 10pm in London is in New York" />

| Ask | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| How long until or since a day | `how many days until march 27th 2027`, `days since 1 jan`, `months until christmas`, `years since 14 feb 2000` |
| How long between two days | `weeks between 1 may and 1 sep`, `days from 1 may to 1 september` |
| Which day a date is | `what day is 14 feb 2027`, `14.02.2027`, `tomorrow` |
| A day counted from another | `3 weeks from today`, `in 10 days`, `10 days ago`, `2 months after 31 jan` |
| The time somewhere else | `time in tokyo`, `tokyo time`, `10pm london in new york`, `9:30 in tokyo`, `3pm est in cet` |
| Percentages | `25% of 340`, `17 is what percent of 340` |
| Currencies | `100 usd in eur`, `$20`, `50 euro to lek` |

Months, weekdays and words such as today or tomorrow work in the language Luft uses, and in English. Questions can also be asked in German, Albanian, Croatian, Serbian and Bosnian, such as `Tage bis zum 27. März`, `sa ditë deri më 27 mars`, `koliko dana do 27. ožujka` or `колико дана до 27. марта`. Numbers follow your region, so `1.234,5` works where a comma marks decimals. A year left out means the next time that day comes, or the last time when you ask how long since.

Places are cities and their time zones as you'd write them, including common abbreviations such as EST, CET and UTC. Currencies use the day's reference rates, which Luft downloads from its own site, luft.computer, the first time you convert a currency on a given day and keeps for offline use. Nothing is downloaded until then, and the request says nothing about you or what you typed.

## Without the taskbar

The same page in Settings can take away the Gust button or the whole taskbar. Without the taskbar, a small corner at the end of the taskbar's edge, the bottom right unless you moved it, keeps the clock and status icons: the icons open quick settings, the clock opens notifications, and the Luft mark, unless you took it away too, opens Gust. Windows switch with <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Tab</kbd>, from [all windows](/windows/task-view/), from **Open** in the palette, or by typing their title into Gust.

In [tablet mode](/desktop/tablet-mode/), the taskbar and its Gust button always come back, since there may be no keyboard to press <kbd>Super</kbd> on.

## When a file doesn't show up

Luft keeps an index of the names and text of files in your home folder. It works at the lowest priority and notes which folders change as you work, then catches up on them every five minutes (fifteen on battery), and when you open Gust or plug in. A file saved a moment ago can take until then to appear. File contents are only read while plugged in; on battery, names stay current and contents wait.

The index leaves out hidden files and folders, other drives, anything a Git repository's `.gitignore` or an `.ignore` file excludes, cache folders such as `node_modules`, and read-only folders. Deleting `~/.cache/kestrel/search` makes it read everything again.
