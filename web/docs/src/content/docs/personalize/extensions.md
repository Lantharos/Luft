Extensions add to the desktop: something on the wallpaper, a tile in quick settings, a button on the taskbar, results in search. Luft comes with one, Seasons, and you can find more, such as [Strays](/personalize/strays/), [Playthings](/personalize/playthings/), [Keystrokes](/personalize/keystrokes/), Soundscapes, Focus, [Reminders](/personalize/reminders/) and Pult, in [Schelf](/apps/schelf/).

**Settings → Extensions** lists them. The switch next to each turns it on or off; opening one shows what it can do, its own settings and **Remove**.

The list under **What it can do** is what the extension says it uses, and what [Schelf](/apps/schelf/) showed you before installing it. It isn't a lock: extensions become part of the desktop itself, so unlike apps they don't run in a sandbox, and one that set out to misbehave could do more than its list says. Extensions in Schelf go through its checks and review before they're published, and are signed so that what you install is what was reviewed. Install extensions from Schelf, from people you trust, the way you would an app you give full access.

If an extension stops working, Luft turns it off by itself and tells you. The same happens to the extensions that were running when the desktop restarted twice in a row after a problem. Turn it back on in Settings once you've updated it, or leave it off.

Removing an extension that came with Luft removes it for your account only; Schelf can bring it back.

## Seasons

Seasons lets rain, snow, autumn leaves, fireflies or mist fall over the wallpaper. They land on windows and the taskbar the way they look, rounded corners and all: rain splashes on their tops and, in the wind, against their sides, snow settles on top, and leaves bump off. Nothing ever falls over what's inside a window, and windows keep whatever is under them dry. With the taskbar in islands, things fall past the gaps between them.

Pick what falls, or let Seasons follow **the weather where you are**: rain, snow or mist when there's some outside, as heavy and as windy as it is. **Follow the season** does the same and, when it's calm, picks by the time of year: snow in winter, showers in spring, fireflies on summer nights and leaves in autumn. Both need location services on to know the weather, which Luft asks of its own site with your position rounded to about ten kilometres and nothing else about you. To stop Luft asking at all, turn off **Weather and currency rates** in Settings, under Privacy.

Rain can also play as **sound**, recorded rain from a drizzle to a downpour with the odd roll of thunder in a storm, and keeps playing behind windows. It goes quiet while you're in a call. Everything builds up when you turn it on and dies away when you turn it off. Rain, snow, leaves and your own pictures **move aside for the pointer** and for your fingers on a touchscreen, parting softly around them on the bare desktop and closing up when they go, a little wider when you move quickly. You can turn that off in Settings → Extensions → Seasons. Seasons stops drawing while a window covers the wallpaper, animations are off or reduced, or the power saver is on.

**My pictures** lets things of your own fall, such as petals, stars or shells. In Settings → Extensions → Seasons, make a set, add up to eight pictures with see-through backgrounds, or short animated GIF or WebP pictures, and choose how many fall, how big and fast they are, the wind, how much they spin, whether they bounce off windows or settle on them for a moment, and a sound for when they land. Seasons keeps its own copies of what you pick, and you can keep several sets.

The Seasons tile in [quick settings](/desktop/quick-settings/) switches it on and off, and its arrow picks what falls, whether it shows on the desktop, and its sound. If you used Rain, Seasons takes its place with your rain settings.

## Strays

[Strays](/personalize/strays/) puts a few small creatures on your desktop that wander across the taskbar and your windows, and that you can pick up, or draw yourself. Its tile in [quick settings](/desktop/quick-settings/) switches them on and off and sets how many.

## Playthings

[Playthings](/personalize/playthings/) hangs lanterns, clouds, a campfire and stars on your wallpaper that react to the pointer and your fingers, and that you can drag wherever you like. Its tile in [quick settings](/desktop/quick-settings/) switches them on and off.

## Keystrokes

[Keystrokes](/personalize/keystrokes/) plays a soft sound as you type and click, from crisp mechanical switches to a typewriter, without seeing what you type. You can make your own set of sounds.

## Tailor

Tailor dresses other apps in Luft's look: the colors of your wallpaper, your fonts, and light and dark. In **Settings → Extensions → Tailor** you'll find the apps it can dress that are on your computer, each with a switch and a line about what it does. Turning one off, or removing Tailor, puts that app back exactly as it was.

- **Spotify** needs [Spicetify](https://spicetify.app) installed first, and follows the desktop while it's open. Spotify has to be installed for your account, which is how Schelf installs it. One that's installed for everyone on the computer can't be changed, and Tailor says so and offers to open Spotify in Schelf.
- **Vesktop** gets a theme that follows the desktop while it's open. The first time, quit it once, from its tray icon too, so the theme can be turned on.
- **Steam** picks up the look each time it opens, and keeps it after updates. Store and community pages stay as they are.

Both the Flatpak and the regular versions of these apps work.

## Reminders

[Reminders](/personalize/reminders/) keeps birthdays, to-dos and goals in the taskbar. Type them the way you'd say them, and they come back until you answer.

## Soundscapes

Soundscapes plays forest, café, fire, waves, wind, distant thunder, rain, white noise and brown noise, on their own or mixed, to focus or fall asleep to. It's in [Schelf](/apps/schelf/).

Its tile in [quick settings](/desktop/quick-settings/) plays and pauses, and its arrow picks a mix, such as Rainy café, Campfire, Seaside, Deep focus or Thunderstorm, opens the mixer with a slider for every sound, and sets the sleep timer. The sleep timer stops it after 15 minutes to an hour and a half, fading out over the last minute. Until then it keeps playing when the screen locks, so you can fall asleep to it.

In Settings → Extensions → Soundscapes you set how loud each sound is, save the mix you made under a name of your own, rename or delete your mixes, and set the overall volume. While music or a video plays in another app it fades out, and it comes back when that stops; turn off **Pause while other things play** to keep it going. It waits the same way while you're in a call, that is while an app uses your microphone or camera, unless you turn off **Pause during calls**. Without a sleep timer, it goes quiet while the screen is locked.

## Focus

Focus is a timer on the taskbar for work sessions. Click it, pick 15, 25 or 45 minutes, an hour or an hour and a half, and start. While it runs, the taskbar shows the minutes left, and under the pointer the seconds, when it ends and how many notifications are waiting, with buttons to pause and end it.

During a session, banners and notification sounds wait. Urgent notifications and apps you let through Do Not Disturb still show, and everything else stays in the notification center. If Soundscapes is installed, the mix you last chose plays during the session and stops after it. When the session ends, a notification says how long you focused and which apps sent what, with a button for another session.

**Super+Alt+F** starts or ends a session. In Settings → Extensions → Focus you choose the length a session starts with, and whether it holds notifications back and plays Soundscapes.

## Pult

Pult puts how much is waiting in your [Pult](https://pult.sh) inboxes on the taskbar, tells you about urgent items with a notification, and finds your items when you search in Start.

To sign in, open Settings → Extensions → Pult and choose **Sign in**. Pult opens in your browser with a code; check that it matches the one in Settings and confirm. The extension can then see your inboxes in every project you're in, but can't change anything, and it keeps its sign-in in your keyring. You can sign it out in Settings, or from Pult under Settings → Security.

Pult checks for new items every minute while you're signed in and the screen is unlocked, every three minutes on battery, and not at all while the power saver is on. Clicking it on the taskbar checks right away. If Pult can't be reached, it says so and tries again by itself, less and less often, or when you choose **Try again**.

Clicking it on the taskbar lists urgent items and every inbox with something waiting, each opening in Pult. Choose whether the number counts everything waiting or only what you haven't read, and whether it hides when nothing waits. Urgent items show even during Do Not Disturb and focus sessions; turn off **Notify about urgent items** if you'd rather just see the count.
