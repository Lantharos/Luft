Schelf is where you get apps. Its catalog is [schelf.store](https://schelf.store): apps and extensions developers publish there, with Flathub's and Fedora's apps alongside them. It also installs command-line tools from Fedora and the AppImages, `.deb` and `.rpm` files you open. System updates, such as the kernel and drivers, live in [Settings](/system/updates/), so Schelf only deals with apps.

## Finding apps

- **Discover** shows popular and recently updated apps, and categories. Apps published on schelf.store come first, then Flathub's, then Fedora's, and an app that's in more than one place shows once. The app at the top is always one you don't have yet, apps you have are marked Installed, and apps that only help another system get ready for Luft, such as Meet Luft, aren't listed.
- **Extensions** lists what you can add to Luft's desktop, such as something drawn on the wallpaper or a quick settings tile, each with a picture of it at work.
- **Search** looks through your installed apps, the catalog, extensions and command-line tools at once. Names that match what you typed come first, and among equal matches schelf.store's own apps come before mirrored ones. It forgives typos while you're online, and searches the catalog on your computer when you're not.
- **App pages** have screenshots, release notes, the size, licence and source, and what the app can use in plain words, broadest access first. Apps from Flathub and Fedora say so under their name. Apps published on schelf.store also say where they come from: whether they were built in public on GitHub Actions, from which repository and commit, with a link to the build.
- **Full access**, on an installed Flatpak app's or AppImage's page, takes the app out of its sandbox, for apps that need it, such as a password manager. [Installing software](/system/software/#full-access) explains what that means.
- The catalog is kept on disk and checked for changes when Schelf opens and every six hours, so browsing works offline.

## Installing and updating

- Downloads queue up, show how much has arrived and what's left, then the install step, and can be cancelled. Before a schelf.store app installs, its confirmation says what it can use and how much it downloads.
- Before an app from schelf.store installs, Schelf shows what it can use and asks you. An app that asks to run outside its sandbox gets the full access confirmation instead. When an update asks for more than you agreed to, or would move an extension out of its sandbox to run inside Luft, it waits under Updates until you look at it again.
- **Installed** lists every app with its size and where it came from. Under System are the [system packages](/system/system-packages/), with the services they run, and **Software sources**, where vendors' package repositories are added and removed.
- **Updates** updates every app in one go. Flatpak runtimes are listed separately, as app platforms.
- Apps from schelf.store and AppImages update by themselves a few minutes after you sign in, and every six hours after that. New versions reach computers in stages over a few days, so a bad release can be stopped before everyone has it.
- Apps from schelf.store get finished versions, the stable channel. When an app also publishes previews, its page lets you switch it to **Beta**, previews of its next version, or **Nightly**, built from its newest code, often every day, which can reach you after only automatic checks. Schelf remembers the choice for that app and only installs versions from the channel you picked. Going back to Stable keeps the version you have until a stable one is newer.
- An installed app's page also sets its name and icon, and how it notifies you: whether it may, its banners and sound, whether it gets through Do Not Disturb, and what it shows on the lock screen.

Opening a `.flatpak`, `.flatpakref`, `.deb`, `.AppImage` or `.rpm` file shows a page explaining it, and where it would go, before anything is installed. A desktop app's `.rpm`, such as 1Password's or Chrome's, becomes a system package, and when it comes with its vendor's repository, Schelf shows that repository and its key and adds it, so the app keeps getting updates. A `.flatpak` file installs its app for your account, along with the runtime it needs from Flathub; it updates only from newer files unless it names a repository of its own. A `.deb` file's app is offered from Flathub when it's there too, and otherwise goes into an Ubuntu container. [Installing software](/system/software/) explains where each kind goes.

## Apps from schelf.store

Everything Schelf downloads from schelf.store is signed, and Schelf checks the signature and every file before it installs anything; [App permissions](/security/app-permissions/#trusting-the-catalog) describes the checks. AppImages run in their [sandbox](/system/software/#full-access) with the access they declared, apps built with Sabine install like Luft's own apps, and extensions are added to your desktop, inside it when the store approved them and in a sandbox otherwise ([how extensions run](/personalize/extensions/)).

Some apps on schelf.store come as installers and archives for each system instead. Schelf installs their AppImage like any other. When an app only has another kind of Linux file, such as a `.deb`, `.rpm` or `.flatpak`, its page offers it to download, and opening the file shows where it would go before anything is installed. Apps with nothing for Linux aren't shown.

An app that isn't listed on schelf.store still has its page there, and a `schelf:app/` link from it opens that page in Schelf.

### Invited versions

Some developers share versions of their apps only with people they invite. To get them, choose **Account** at the bottom of the sidebar and sign in: Schelf opens schelf.store in your browser and shows a code, and once you approve it there, the account lists what you're invited to. Each invited channel then appears on its app's page next to Stable, and choosing it installs and updates that app from it, checked like any other release. The sign-in is kept in [Luft Keyring](/security/keyring/), and **Sign out** in the same place removes it.

When a developer takes an invitation back, Schelf tells you once, keeps what you installed and goes back to the app's public versions.

schelf.store can pull an app that turns out to be harmful. Schelf checks for that every hour: a pulled app can't start any more, and its page says why and offers to remove it. Your files stay until you do. For a version with a known problem, the page shows a warning instead.

### Your own projects

If you publish on schelf.store, choosing your name at the bottom of the sidebar switches Schelf to your projects, and **Back to the store** switches back. Each project shows what's live on each channel, its releases with every check and why one went to review, downloads and updates over time, its channels and rollouts, the people invited to its invite-only channels, and its files and command-line tools with their install lines to copy. Below the projects are your publisher's command-line tools, members, verification and download domain. When you belong to more than one publisher, choose its name at the top to switch.

Schelf does what the `schelf` command can do by itself, such as pausing a rollout, releasing it to everyone or taking back an invitation. Rolling back, inviting someone or changing who's a member opens schelf.store in your browser to approve it first, and Schelf shows the result once you have. Verification and anything else only the dashboard does open it there.

## Command-line tools

Search for a tool, such as `ffmpeg` or `htop`, and Schelf installs it from Fedora into a Toolbox container. Its commands work in [Tern](/apps/tern/) like any other, and its updates appear under Updates.

Tools published on schelf.store install from the terminal with `schelf add` and their address, such as `alice/notes`, into `~/.local/bin`. Each is one program, checked against what schelf.store signed for it, and it runs with everything you can do, so Schelf says who publishes it before it goes ahead. They show up under Installed and update with your apps.

## In the terminal

`schelf install`, `remove`, `search`, `update`, `list`, `info`, `trust`, `untrust` and `repo` do the same from [Tern](/apps/tern/), and `sch` is short for `schelf`. The same command puts your own apps on schelf.store, with `schelf init`, `check` and `publish`. While the app is open, what you start in the terminal shows up in its queue too. [Installing software](/system/software/#in-the-terminal) has the details.
