The taskbar sits along the bottom of the screen, or wherever you [arrange it](#arranging-the-taskbar). From left to right in its middle are the Luft mark, which opens [Start](/desktop/start-and-search/), and your apps. On the right are the tray, the status icons that open [Quick settings](/desktop/quick-settings/), and the clock, which opens [Notifications](/desktop/notifications/).

<Shot name="taskbar" alt="The taskbar with pinned apps, status icons and the clock" />

## Apps

Pinned apps come first, then any other app that's running.

- **Dots** under an app show how many windows it has, up to four. The app you're using has wider dots.
- **A badge** counts unread messages, for apps that report them.
- **A progress bar** replaces the dots while an app reports progress, such as a download.
- **A flash** means an app wants your attention. It flashes three times, then stays lit until you look.

Clicking an app opens it, switches to its window, or minimizes the window if it's already in front. When an app has several windows, clicking shows them all to choose from.

Games you start from Steam get a button of their own, with the game's name and icon, rather than sitting under Steam. Pin one and clicking it starts the game through Steam.

Hover over an app, or press the arrow key that points away from the taskbar's edge, such as <kbd>Up</kbd> on the bottom, to see **live previews** of its windows, each with a close button.

## Pinning

- Drag an app from Start onto the taskbar to pin it.
- Drag pinned apps along the taskbar to reorder them.
- Right-click an app for **Pin to taskbar** or **Unpin from taskbar**.

## Right-click menus

On a touchscreen, touch and hold for the same menu.

| Where | What's in it |
| --- | --- |
| An app | Open, New window, the app's own actions, each of its windows, minimize and maximize, pin or unpin, and close |
| The taskbar | Show desktop, System monitor, Edit taskbar, and Taskbar settings |
| The status icons | Network and Sound settings |
| The clock | Date and time settings |

## The tray

Apps that live in the background, such as chat or sync apps, show up in the tray, a single button with up to four of their icons. Click it to see them all, and click one for its menu.

## Show desktop

The far end of the taskbar, past the clock, is a thin strip. Hover over it to fade every window away for a look at the desktop, and click it to minimize everything; click again to bring them back.

## Arranging the taskbar

The taskbar is made of parts: Start, your apps, the tray, the status icons, the clock, and anything extensions add, such as what's playing. Parts sit together in islands, and each island goes to one of twelve spots: the start, middle or end of any edge of the screen. Islands along the top and bottom run across, those on the left and right run down.

Right-click the taskbar and choose **Edit taskbar**, or choose **Edit on the desktop** in Settings. The desktop dims and the twelve spots show.

- Drag an island by the small handle beside it to move it to another spot. Dropping it on another island swaps the two.
- Drag a part, such as Start or the clock, into another island, or onto an empty spot to give it an island of its own. Drag it back to put them together again.
- Drag a part along its island to change the order.
- With the keyboard, <kbd>Tab</kbd> picks an island or one of its parts, and the arrow keys move it.

The taskbar shows every change as you make it. **Done** or <kbd>Enter</kbd> keeps it, **Reset to default** brings back the taskbar Luft starts with, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> puts everything back as it was.

<Shot name="taskbar-edit" alt="Editing the taskbar, with the desktop dimmed and the spots along the edges" />

Settings has the same arrangement on its preview, where you can drag islands and parts too, and a few layouts to start from:

| Layout | What you get |
| --- | --- |
| Classic | One bar along the bottom, with Start and your apps on the left |
| Centered dock | Start and your apps in the middle of the bottom, the clock at its end |
| macOS-like | A dock at the bottom and the status icons and clock at the top right |
| Side dock | Your apps down the left edge and the clock at the bottom right |

Islands next to the same corner never overlap: the one on the top or bottom keeps the corner, and the one on the side moves along its edge to make room. Maximized windows keep a gap from every edge with an island, and their corners facing an island stay rounded. Start, quick settings and notifications open beside the island their button is in, and notification banners appear next to the clock.

The arrangement shines with the Separate shape. Edge to edge and Floating keep the taskbar as one bar, along the edge of your apps, with every island at its place along it; moving an island to another edge in Edit taskbar moves the whole bar there.

## Options

Right-click the taskbar and choose **Taskbar settings**, or open Settings, Taskbar and Start.

<Shot name="settings-taskbar" alt="The taskbar's options in Settings, with a preview" />

The page opens on a preview of your taskbar, which you can arrange right there. Shape, look, size and layouts sit under the preview; **Start** and **More options** hold the rest.

| Option | Choices |
| --- | --- |
| Style | Start as a Menu next to the taskbar, or as [Gust](/desktop/gust/), a search bar in the middle of the screen |
| Show the Start button | When off, <kbd>Super</kbd> still opens Start |
| Search the web with | DuckDuckGo, Google, Bing, Brave Search, Ecosia, Startpage or Kagi |
| Show the taskbar | When off, the clock and status icons stay in a corner, see [without the taskbar](/desktop/gust/#without-the-taskbar) |
| Look | Glass (blurred), Solid, Transparent, or Accent (tinted glass) |
| Shape | Edge to edge; Floating, with rounded ends and a gap around it; or Separate, where each island floats on its own. Maximized windows keep the same gap from a floating or separate taskbar |
| Size | Small, Medium or Large |
| Hide automatically | Never, Always, or When a window touches it |
| Show pinned apps | When off, pinned apps move into Start instead |
| Only list windows on the current workspace | Each workspace's taskbar shows only its own windows |
| Show on every display | With several displays, a taskbar on each one |
| Only list windows on the same display | Each display's taskbar shows only its own windows |

<Shot name="taskbar-separate" alt="The Separate taskbar, with Start and the apps in the middle and the status icons and clock apart at the end" />

The taskbar sits on your wallpaper, so it takes its light or dark look from the part of the wallpaper behind it: light over a bright beach or snow, dark over a night sky, and your light or dark style when the wallpaper is in between. Start, quick settings and your apps keep following your style. The clock and status icons pick a light or dark ink, whichever reads better against what's behind the taskbar, so they stay clear on any wallpaper.

On the left or right, apps stack from the top and the clock shows the time over the date. The taskbar's menus open beside the button you used.

On a convertible folded into a tablet, and on a pen display or touch monitor, the taskbar moves to the bottom and becomes a thin strip that you swipe up for a touch-sized taskbar, see [Tablet mode](/desktop/tablet-mode/).

With several displays, each one gets its own taskbar with Start, its apps and the clock, arranged the same way. The tray, status icons and privacy indicator stay on the main display. A taskbar steps aside on a display where an app is full screen.
