Snapping is for floating windows. While [window tiling](/windows/tiling/) is on, windows arrange themselves and snapping is off.

## Snapping with the mouse

Drag a window by its title bar:

- to the left or right edge to fill that half of the screen
- to the top edge to maximize it
- to an edge near a corner, or to the top edge near a side, to fill that quarter

Drag a snapped window away and it goes back to the size it had before.

## Snapping with the keyboard

<kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Left</kbd> and <kbd>Right</kbd> snap the window to a half, <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Up</kbd> maximizes it, and <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Down</kbd> restores it.

## Snap layouts

Press <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> to choose where the window you're using goes, from six layouts:

- two halves
- two thirds and one third
- three thirds
- four quarters
- one half and two quarters
- a quarter, a half and a quarter

<Shot name="snap-layouts" alt="The snap layouts above the taskbar" />

Click the spot you want in a layout. The picker sits right next to the taskbar.

## Snap groups

Windows snapped side by side on the same workspace, without overlapping, become a group. Switching to one of them from the taskbar, Alt+Tab or All windows brings the others back too, so a layout you set up stays together.
