Luft isn't a collection of packages that update one by one. Each version of the system is one sealed image, built and signed in one go, and an update swaps the whole system for the next version. Your files, settings and apps live apart from it and stay as they are.

This means an update either happens completely or not at all. A restart in the middle of downloading leaves the system you had, and if a new version doesn't start, Luft goes back by itself.

## Getting an update

Luft looks for updates every day and downloads them in the background. When one is ready, a notification says so.

1. **Settings, System** shows the new version, what's new in it, when it was released and how much it downloads. Only the parts of the system that changed are downloaded.
2. **Restart to install.** The download waits until you restart or shut down.
3. The **Restart** and **Power off** dialogs have an **Install version …** box, ticked unless the battery is low. Leave it unticked and the update simply waits for another time.

The version you had stays on the computer as the previous version.

After a restart to install an update, the apps you had open come back once you sign in, with their windows where you left them. Luft's own apps also come back with what you had open in them, such as your folders in Rover or the page you were on in Settings. To have them come back after every restart and sign-out too, turn on **Reopen apps when I sign in** in Settings, under Apps.

In **Automatic updates** you choose how often Luft looks (every day, every week, or only when you check) and whether it downloads in the background. Changing it asks for an administrator's password. The choice is the same for everyone on the computer, and Luft looks even when nobody is signed in. It waits while the computer runs on its battery or uses a metered connection, such as a phone's hotspot, and looks once it's plugged in or on another network.

## Going back

If something in a new version doesn't suit you, **Go back to the previous version** in Settings, under System, makes the version before it start next time. Your files and settings stay as they are. While going back is queued, you can still choose to stay.

You can also go back while the computer starts: in the boot menu, which shows itself next to Windows and otherwise appears when you hold any key, pick the version under **Previous versions**.

## When an update doesn't start

A new version gets three tries to reach the login screen. If it doesn't get there, Luft starts the version that last worked, explains what happened before the login screen, and doesn't offer that update again; the next release replaces it.

## Releases and nightly builds

| | Comes | Follows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Releases | When a version is ready | `ghcr.io/lantharos/luft:1` |
| Nightly builds | Every night, when something changed | `ghcr.io/lantharos/luft:1-nightly` |

Releases are numbered Luft 1.1, 1.2 and on, and the number before the dot changes with each new Fedora release Luft moves to. A nightly build is named after the release it leads to, such as Luft 1.2 Nightly 21, the 21st nightly build on the way to 1.2. Settings, under System, About shows which one you have and the day it was built.

A computer follows the channel it was installed from: one installed from a nightly stick keeps getting nightly builds. **Release channel** in Settings, under System, shows the channel your computer is on, and **Switch** moves it to the other one. Until the first stable version is out there's nothing to switch to, so the button isn't there. Luft asks you to confirm, then for an administrator's password, and keeps your choice of the standard or NVIDIA image.

Switching never installs an older version than the one you have. A stable release is a nightly build that was tested and promoted, so when you move from the nightly builds to stable, your computer keeps getting nightly updates until the next stable release that's at least as new as the build it has, then stays on stable. When that release is the build you already have, nothing is downloaded. Moving to the nightly builds happens right away when they have a version at least as new as yours; otherwise your computer keeps its version until they do.

While your computer waits to move, Settings says so, and **Switch** keeps it on the channel it's on instead. When Luft can't reach the update server, Settings says it couldn't check whether a stable version is out, and you can't switch to stable until it can.

Nightly builds get the least testing: nobody has used one for long before it reaches you. When something breaks or looks wrong, Settings › System › Report a problem opens a new issue with your version filled in.

Every image is signed with Luft's key, and Luft refuses an update that isn't. It also refuses one that's older than the version you have, so nobody can hand your computer an old version with holes that were fixed since. Going back to the previous version is always your choice. [Keys, signatures and the build](/security/keys/) shows how to check an image's signatures yourself, and [Updates and releases](/developers/system/updates/) how the updater works.

## Updating firmware and apps

- **Firmware** for the computer and its devices also comes through Settings, under System, when the maker publishes it. Most firmware updates finish at the next restart.
- **Apps** aren't part of the system image. Settings points to [Schelf](/apps/schelf/), which updates them.

## From the command line

| Command | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `bootc status` | Shows the running version, a downloaded one, and the previous one |
| `sudo bootc upgrade` | Downloads the newest version for the next restart |
| `sudo bootc rollback` | Starts the previous version next time |
